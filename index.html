Projects

01

imagine

YouTube 3:04 5 members
Director · Scriptwriter · Producer · Animator

A story about two dots questioning who they are — shifting between identity, perspective, and the quiet weight of small things — until they discover they are the dots above the two i's in the word imagine.

I came up with the concept and wrote the script, but the bigger challenge was figuring out how five people could make something together without losing coherence. My solution was an anijam structure — one person, one medium — so each collaborator could work in their strongest form. I built the storyboard and animatic first, giving the team a shared skeleton to work from, then stepped back to let each part breathe.

UX Thinking

Making this film taught me that the hardest part of any collaborative project isn't the craft — it's alignment. Before anyone picks up a camera or opens a software, everyone needs to understand the same goal. It also taught me something about pacing: how to distribute work in a way that's sustainable, and how to recognise when pushing harder stops being productive. Both lessons, I think, matter just as much in design as they do in filmmaking.

02

Healing Rituals

YouTube 0:34 Solo
Stop-Motion · Sand Animation · Complete end-to-end

A wordless meditation on personal rituals — tea, music, stirring coffee, boiling water — rendered through sand, powder, pepper, and salt. This was my first complete stop-motion project, developed independently from script to final frame. The material choice was intentional: impermanence and texture as metaphor for the fragile, sensory nature of self-care. No narration. The image carries everything.

UX Thinking

Working without words means every visual choice has to do more work. The texture of sand, the rhythm of a pour, the pace of a cut — these aren't decoration, they're the whole language. What I didn't realise at the time was that this is exactly what good interface design does too: guiding people through something without them noticing they're being guided.

03

Simulation Game Visual Design

Solo Project · Visual Design · Character Design · World-building

A visual and character design system for an original simulation game concept. The project focused on building a coherent world through visual language: colour palette, character archetypes, and environmental tone designed to communicate the game's identity at a glance.

Working on a game concept meant constantly thinking about the player's experience : what story do these visuals tell before a single word of text is read?

UX Thinking

Game visual systems function as UX systems in disguise. Colour carries meaning, character design suggests personality, and spatial layout directs attention and flow. This project made me realise I had already been practicing visual communication design in a context where meaning is constantly shaped by user perception.

Design Process

Why this portfolio
looks the way it does

Building this site was itself a UX exercise. I treated it as a real design problem: a specific user, a tight time constraint, and a clear goal.

01 · Problem
An audience with no time

This portfolio is designed for reviewers who are evaluating many applicants under tight time constraints. The challenge is not simply to present work, but to communicate who I am and how I think in a way that can be understood quickly, often within seconds of scanning.

02 · Target Users
Reviewers & admissions staff

Not reading in depth, but moving quickly, looking for signals of clarity, intent, and design thinking rather than exhaustive explanation.

03 · Goals
Rapid comprehension of intent

The goal of the portfolio is to allow viewers to understand my work and thinking with minimal cognitive effort. This includes establishing clarity early, supporting quick visual comprehension of projects, and ensuring that my design thinking is visible even in a brief scan.

04 · Design Decisions
Why these choices?
  • Single page — no navigation friction; scroll is universal
  • Serif + mono — editorial warmth meets technical precision
  • Warm paper tones — references print; signals craft sensibility
  • UX notes per project — makes implicit thinking explicit
05 · Reflection
What I would do differently with more time

User testing — even one round with a classmate acting as "admissions reviewer" — would reveal whether the reading order actually works. I would also add a more detailed breakdown of my production process for imagine, including original storyboard sketches and team coordination documents.
